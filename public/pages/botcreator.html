1What is a bot?

A bot is a pretend person that sits in a room with its own textbox and follows rules you wrote. Everyone can see it is a bot - it wears a BOT badge with your name on it. It types letter by letter, just like a person.

Every rule is one sentence with three parts:

WHEN someone types !fish ONLY IF the checks pass (you can skip this part) DO say "You caught a fish!"
2Your first bot in one minute
  1. Press Start from an idea and answer the questions, or open the ready-made Dice bot.
  2. Look at its one rule: WHEN someone types !roll → DO say something.
  3. In the Test room on the right, type !roll in your box and stop typing.
  4. Watch the rule light up and the bot type in its own box. That's it, that's a bot.
  5. Change the say text. Test again. It changed instantly.
  6. Press Save, then Send to a room.
3The WHEN part (what wakes your bot up)

One important thing first: Talkomatic has no send button. So how does the bot know you finished? It waits until your last line stops changing for about 2 seconds, then reads that line. Press Enter for a new line when you want to say a new thing to the bot.

someone types !word The classic. It can sit anywhere in your line: !roll and "please !roll" both work. Words typed after it (like !guess 7 fast) become {word1}, {word2}... up to {word8}. someone says a phrase Fires when the phrase is anywhere in the line. If the phrase is "pizza", then "i love pizza so much" wakes the bot up. someone says the bot's name Someone wrote your bot's name in their line. Nice for "hey FishBot". someone joins the room A person walks in. Perfect for welcome bots. (Bots never trigger other bots - only people count.) someone leaves the room A person walks out. every X minutes A clock, no person needed. Shortest is 2 minutes. In the Test room, press "Ring the timers" instead of waiting. Remember: the bot goes home about a minute after YOU leave Talkomatic, and its timers go with it.

Admin commands (the "who can trigger it" dropdown)

Next to every WHEN there is a second dropdown: anyone can trigger it (normal) or only me (admin). Set it to "only me" and that rule ignores everybody except you, the person who sent the bot to the room. That is how you give your bot admin commands: a !say rule that makes it talk, a !clear rule that wipes its box, a !gohome rule that sends it home - all commands only you can use.

It is matched by your Talkomatic session, not your name, so someone renaming themselves to your name gets nothing. In the Test room YOU count as the owner, so your admin rules fire when you test them (and Testy can't trigger them). Open the ready-made Admin commands bot to see a working set.

4The DO part (what your bot does)
say The bot types a message into its box, letter by letter, replacing what was there. This is where you use magic words (next section). Up to 1000 letters. add a line below Like say, but UNDER what the bot already said instead of erasing it. A greeter can stack "mohd arrived" and "xyerv left"; a tic tac toe bot can keep its board on screen. When the box fills up, the oldest lines scroll away. wait Pause before the next action. Timing makes jokes work: say "Shaking the 8 ball..." then wait 2 seconds, then say the answer. remember Write something down in the bot's memory, under a name you pick, like prize or coins. See the Memory section, it is the part that makes games possible. change a memory Do math on a memory: add, take away, multiply by, or divide by a number. Counting points, doubling coins, halving a score - all this one action. pick random Put a random pick into a memory. Give it choices (rock, paper, scissors) or a number range (1-100). repeat Runs everything above it again, up to 5 times in total. One per rule. A say above a repeat becomes a chant; a random + say above one becomes three different rolls. erase the box The bot wipes its own textbox clean. leave the room The bot stands up and goes home.

Actions run in order, top to bottom - drag a block by its grip to reorder. If a rule picks a random prize and then says it, "pick random" has to be above the say.

5Magic words

Write these (with the curly brackets) inside anything your bot says, checks, or remembers. The bot swaps them for the real thing at that moment:

{name} The person who woke the bot up. Say "Hi {name}!" and Sara reads "Hi Sara!" {word1} {word2} {word3} The words typed after a command. If someone types !guess 7 please, then {word1} is "7" and {word2} is "please". {words} ALL the words after the command, together. {line} The whole line the person typed. {memory:coins} What is written in the memory called "coins" (works for any memory name). {mymemory:coins} That person's OWN "coins" memory. Sara's coins, when Sara asked. See Memory below. {rand:1-6} A fresh random number from 1 to 6, different every time. Any range works: {rand:1-100}. {pick:red|green|blue} A fresh random choice from your list. Split the choices with the | line. {newline} Starts a new line in the middle of a message. (Pressing Enter inside a say box does the same thing.) {commands} Every PUBLIC !command this bot answers to, one per line (admin-only commands stay off this list). Put it in a !help say and the list never goes stale. {ownercommands} Every admin-only !command, one per line. Put it in an admin-only !admin rule so only you see the private list. {owner} The name of the person running the bot - you. "I only take orders from {owner}." {runtime} How long the bot has been in its room, like "2h 14m". Made for !info commands. {bot} Your bot's own name. {room} The room's name. {humans} How many people are in the room (bots not counted). {time} The time right now (UTC).

In the editor there is a ✨ button next to every say box - it drops these in for you so you never have to remember the spelling.

6Memory (how games work)

A memory is a little labelled box the bot keeps. You invent the label. There are two kinds, and the difference matters:

shared by everyone ONE box for the whole room. Good for: the answer to the current quiz question, the bot's last rock-paper-scissors throw, a group score. each person their own EVERY person gets their own box with the same label. Sara's "coins" and Omar's "coins" are different boxes. Good for: points, coins, personal bests, "have I welcomed this person before".

Memories start at 0, stick around while your bot is deployed, and are saved with your bot. In the Test room, the "What it remembers" panel shows them changing live.

You never have to type the curly brackets: the MEMORIES part of the palette lists every memory your bot has, plus a + new memory button. Click into a say or check box, then click a memory chip and it drops right in.

7The ONLY IF part (checks)

Checks make bots smart. A rule only runs when EVERY check passes. Each check compares two things: is exactly, is not, is bigger than, is smaller than, or contains.

"Is exactly" means exactly (capital letters don't matter, but spelling does). Use magic words in either side:

WHEN someone types !buy ONLY IF {mymemory:coins} is bigger than 9 DO count up coins by -10, then say "Sold! You have {mymemory:coins} coins left."

Want an "otherwise"? Make a second rule for the same command with the opposite check ("is smaller than 10") that says "You can't afford it yet!"

8Recipes (how do I make...)

A quiz with points

  1. Rule 1: WHEN someone types !quiz → DO say "What has keys but can't open doors? Answer with !answer"
  2. Rule 2: WHEN someone types !answer → ONLY IF {word1} is exactly piano → DO count up points (each person their own) by 1, then say "Right! {name} has {mymemory:points} points 🏆"
  3. Rule 3: WHEN someone types !answer → ONLY IF {word1} is not piano → DO say "Nope, try again!"

A secret password door

  1. Rule 1: WHEN someone types !door → ONLY IF {word1} is exactly banana → DO say "🚪 The door swings open for {name}..."
  2. Rule 2: WHEN someone types !door → ONLY IF {word1} is not banana → DO say "Wrong password."

A shop

Give people coins with a command (change a memory), check coins with "is bigger than" before selling (see the ONLY IF example above), and show a menu with a plain say rule for !shop. Open the ready-made Fishing bot - it is a whole tiny economy you can copy.

A doubling machine

One rule: WHEN someone types !double → DO remember n = {word1}, then change n multiply by 2, then say "{word1} doubled is {memory:n}!" Open the ready-made Calculator bot to see it working, halving included.

A bot that argues back differently every time

One rule: WHEN someone says the bot's name → DO say {pick:Who, me?|I heard that.|Leave me out of this.|{name}, please.}

A reminder bot

WHEN every 10 minutes → DO say "💧 Water break! It is {time}." (Test it with the "Ring the timers" button - no waiting.)

Admin controls (commands only you can use)

  1. Rule 1: WHEN someone types !say, set to only me (admin) → DO say {words}. Now !say good morning everyone makes the bot talk, but only when YOU type it.
  2. Rule 2: WHEN someone types !gohome, set to only me (admin) → DO say "Off I go. 👋", wait 2 seconds, leave the room.
  3. Rule 3: WHEN someone types !admin, set to only me (admin) → DO say "{ownercommands}" - your private command list, on demand.

The ready-made Admin commands bot is exactly this, plus !clear.

9"My bot isn't answering!"
Old line The bot only reads your LAST line. A command sitting higher up in your box is old news; type it again at the bottom. Too fast The bot reads your line after it stops changing for ~2 seconds. Give it a beat. (The Test room answers instantly.) Spelling "is exactly" means exactly. !fish and !fishing are different commands. Curly brackets If your bot literally says "{wrod1}", the magic word is misspelled - the bot leaves typos visible so you can find them. Use the ✨ button. Bot pace Bots take a short breath between messages and can't spam. If you queue 10 says, they come out one at a time. It vanished Your bot goes home when: you leave Talkomatic, its room empties, the room votes it out, or a moderator stops it. Only works for me That rule's "who can trigger it" dropdown is set to only me (admin). That is the point - flip it back to "anyone can trigger it" if everyone should be able to use it.
10The rules (read this once)

Bots follow the same rules people do. What they say is filtered by each viewer's own automod setting, exactly like what people type. A room can vote a bot out. Rooms have bot seats by size (1 for every 5 people they can hold, up to 5), you can run one bot at a time, and whatever your bot does counts as YOU doing it - so make things people will like having around. Moderators can see every bot and its owner, and can turn any bot off.

Drive a bot with real code instead of blocks. Your program connects over Socket.IO, takes an ordinary seat with a BOT badge, and talks the same protocol the site itself uses. Node, Python, or anything else with a Socket.IO client. Everything is documented on this page, and both example bots at the bottom run as-is.

1What a coded bot can do

There are two ways to make a bot. The block editor on this page (tier 1) runs your bot on the Talkomatic server itself: no code, no hosting, and it follows you around. A coded bot (tier 2) is a program you run, on your own machine or server, connected from the outside. It stays up as long as your program does.

A coded bot can do everything a person at a keyboard can:

  • Sign in with a name, a location line, and an avatar.
  • List the public rooms, create rooms, join rooms (including semi-private ones with the code).
  • Read everyone's textbox live, keystroke by keystroke, exactly as people see it.
  • Type into its own box: instantly, or letter by letter like the hosted bots.
  • Show the "typing" indicator.
  • See people join and leave, see votes change, and vote itself.
  • Read the public REST endpoints (server status, config, avatar list).

What it cannot do: read rooms it has not joined, join a room that disallows bots or is already at its bot limit (rooms get 1 bot seat per 5 people they hold, up to 5), or escape moderation. Everyone sees the BOT badge, rooms can vote a bot out, staff can disconnect any bot, and whatever your bot does counts as you doing it.

2Get a token

Every automated connection needs a bot token. You request one from code, not from a browser (requests that look like a browser are refused on purpose):

terminal
curl -X POST https://classic.talkomatic.co/api/v1/bot-tokens/request \
  -H "User-Agent: my-bot/1.0"
response
{
  "token": "tk_a1b2c3...64 hex characters",
  "expiresIn": 2592000000,
  "expiresAt": "2026-09-23T12:00:00.000Z",
  "usage": { "rateLimit": "500 req/min", "headers": "Authorization: Bearer {token}" }
}

The rules around tokens, worth knowing before anything surprises you:

30 days How long a token lasts from the moment it is made. 3 per IP At most 3 live tokens per IP address at once. 3 requests/hour The request endpoint allows 3 calls per hour per IP. A 4th is blocked for an hour, so do not request a token on every start. Save it to a file and reuse it (section 4). Dies on restart Tokens live in the server's memory. Every Talkomatic update or restart forgets them all. This is the top cause of "my bot suddenly can't connect": the token is gone and must be re-requested. Section 4 makes that automatic. Not in browsers Tokens are for programs. Browser page code never needs one, and a browser presenting a token is refused.

Check any token's state (also the cleanest way to test whether a saved token is still good):

terminal
curl https://classic.talkomatic.co/api/v1/bot-tokens/info \
  -H "User-Agent: my-bot/1.0" \
  -H "Authorization: Bearer tk_your_token_here"

A good token answers 200 with createdAt, lastUsed, expiresAt and a uses count. A dead one answers 401.

3Five minute start

The smallest bot that does something. Paste your token in, put a real room id in (the 6-digit number in the room page's address), run it, and type !ping in that room:

quickstart.js
// npm install socket.io-client        (Node 18 or newer)
const { io } = require("socket.io-client");

const socket = io("https://classic.talkomatic.co", {
  transports: ["websocket"],              // the server speaks websocket only
  auth: { token: "tk_paste_your_token_here" },
});

socket.on("connect", () => {
  socket.emit("join lobby", { username: "MyFirstBot", location: "Bot" });
});

socket.on("signin status", (s) => {
  if (s.isSignedIn) socket.emit("join room", { roomId: "123456" });
});

socket.on("room joined", (d) => console.log("Seated in", d.roomName));

// Everyone's box streams in live. Answer anyone whose line becomes "!ping".
socket.on("chat update", (d) => {
  const line = (d.diff.text || "").split("\n").filter((l) => l.trim()).pop() || "";
  if (line.trim() === "!ping") {
    socket.emit("chat update", {
      diff: { type: "full-replace", text: "pong, " + d.username + "!" },
    });
  }
});

socket.on("afk warning", () => socket.emit("afk response")); // keep the seat
socket.on("error", (e) => console.log("Server said:", e?.error?.message));

Two things in there deserve a look before you build bigger. The chat update handler fires on every keystroke, not once per message, because Talkomatic has no send button: section 9 shows the proper way to read lines. And the afk warning line at the bottom is what keeps your bot from quietly vanishing after 3 minutes: section 10 explains.

4Tokens that renew themselves

Hardcoded tokens die twice: after 30 days, and every time the server restarts. The fix is one function that your bot calls on every connection attempt. It reuses the saved token while the server still accepts it, and requests a fresh one the moment it is refused:

Node
const fs = require("fs");

const SERVER = "https://classic.talkomatic.co";
const TOKEN_FILE = "talkomatic-token.json";

// Reuse the saved token while the server still accepts it. If it expired,
// or the server restarted and forgot it, request a fresh one and save it.
async function getToken() {
  try {
    const saved = JSON.parse(fs.readFileSync(TOKEN_FILE, "utf8"));
    const res = await fetch(SERVER + "/api/v1/bot-tokens/info", {
      headers: { Authorization: "Bearer " + saved.token, "User-Agent": "my-bot/1.0" },
    });
    if (res.ok) return saved.token;
  } catch (err) {}
  const res = await fetch(SERVER + "/api/v1/bot-tokens/request", {
    method: "POST",
    headers: { "User-Agent": "my-bot/1.0" },
  });
  if (!res.ok) throw new Error("Token request failed: " + res.status);
  const data = await res.json();
  fs.writeFileSync(TOKEN_FILE, JSON.stringify(data, null, 2));
  console.log("Fresh token saved, good until", data.expiresAt);
  return data.token;
}

Wire it into the connection with an auth callback instead of a plain object. Socket.IO runs the callback on every attempt, including automatic reconnects, so a token that died while your bot was running heals on the next retry without a restart:

Node
const socket = io(SERVER, {
  transports: ["websocket"],
  // Runs on every attempt, first connect and every reconnect alike.
  auth: (cb) => getToken().then((token) => cb({ token })).catch(() => cb({})),
});

socket.on("connect_error", (e) => console.log("Connect refused:", e.message));

The same function in Python (the run loop in section 12 calls it on every attempt):

Python
import json
import requests

SERVER = "https://classic.talkomatic.co"
TOKEN_FILE = "talkomatic-token.json"
UA = {"User-Agent": "my-bot/1.0"}

# Reuse the saved token while the server still accepts it. If it expired,
# or the server restarted and forgot it, request a fresh one and save it.
def get_token():
    try:
        with open(TOKEN_FILE) as f:
            saved = json.load(f)
        r = requests.get(SERVER + "/api/v1/bot-tokens/info", timeout=10,
                         headers={**UA, "Authorization": "Bearer " + saved["token"]})
        if r.ok:
            return saved["token"]
    except (OSError, ValueError, requests.RequestException):
        pass
    r = requests.post(SERVER + "/api/v1/bot-tokens/request", timeout=10, headers=UA)
    r.raise_for_status()
    data = r.json()
    with open(TOKEN_FILE, "w") as f:
        json.dump(data, f)
    print("Fresh token saved, good until", data["expiresAt"])
    return data["token"]

Because the check goes through /bot-tokens/info first, a healthy saved token costs one cheap read and no request slot. The 3-per-hour request limit only ever gets touched when the token is truly dead.

5Connecting

The server speaks Socket.IO v4 over websocket only. There is no HTTP long-polling fallback, so every client must say so explicitly, and the token goes in the handshake's auth field:

Node
const socket = io("https://classic.talkomatic.co", {
  transports: ["websocket"],
  auth: { token: "tk_your_token_here" },
});
Python
sio.connect("https://classic.talkomatic.co",
            auth={"token": "tk_your_token_here"},
            transports=["websocket"])

Send an honest User-Agent like my-bot/1.0 on the HTTP calls, and do not dress your program up in browser headers. The server sorts connections into "browser" and "program" by their headers: programs need a token, browsers must not have one. A program wearing a fake browser User-Agent lands in the browser rules and gets refused for carrying a token.

When a connection is refused, the reason arrives in connect_error:

Bot token required No token reached the server. Check the field name: it is auth.token. Invalid bot token Expired, mistyped, or forgotten in a server restart. Request a fresh one (section 4 automates this). Bot tokens not allowed in browsers The handshake looked like a browser. Drop the fake browser headers. Too many connections One IP can hold 8 sockets at once, and your own browser tabs on the same network count. Close some. IP rate limit exceeded More than 20 connection attempts in 15 seconds from one IP. Back off for 30 seconds. A reconnect loop with no delay does this to itself. IP blocked The IP is banned from Talkomatic. The error's data carries the ban details.

The server pings every socket every 25 seconds and drops connections that stay silent for 60. Socket.IO clients answer pings automatically, so this only bites when your process blocks its own event loop for a minute (a busy while-loop, a stuck synchronous call). Keep long work off the socket's thread.

6Signing in

A connected socket is nobody until it signs in. Send join lobby and wait for signin status:

Node
socket.emit("join lobby", {
  username: "MarketBot",   // up to 15 characters
  location: "the docks",   // up to 20, shows under the name; defaults to "On The Web"
  avatar: { preset: 3 },   // optional; ids from GET /api/v1/avatars
});

socket.on("signin status", (s) => {
  // { isSignedIn: true, username, location, userId, isBot: true, ... }
  console.log("I am", s.username, "with id", s.userId);
});

Names go through the same door people use, so all of this is refused:

  • Names on the word filter, names containing a link or an IP address.
  • Guest-style names (guest123, anonymous, someone, unknown).
  • Reserved names and close lookalikes (talkomatic, admin, mod, dev, mohd).
  • Exotic unicode. Ordinary letters, numbers and punctuation only.

A refusal arrives as an error event (or validation_error with a per-field breakdown), and you stay signed out. There is no reserved-name protection between bots and people beyond that list: pick a name that says what the bot is.

The userId you get belongs to this connection's session. Connect again and you are somebody new. Nothing follows the old id around, but room bans stick to it, so a vote-kicked bot that reconnects for a new id and walks back in is ban evasion. Do not build that.

7Rooms

Finding one

Node
socket.emit("get rooms");

socket.on("initial rooms", (rooms) => {
  for (const r of rooms) {
    // r: { id, name, type, userCount, capacity, isFull, allowBots,
    //      locked, users: [{ id, username, location, isBotUser? }, ...] }
    console.log(r.id, r.name, r.userCount + "/" + r.capacity,
                r.allowBots ? "" : "(no bots)");
  }

While you sit in the lobby (signed in, not in a room) the same list keeps arriving as lobby update whenever it changes. Private rooms are never listed; you can still join one if you know its id.

Making one

Node
socket.emit("create room", {
  name: "fish market",        // up to 25 characters, must be unused
  type: "public",             // or "semi-private" (+ accessCode) or "private"
  // accessCode: "123456",    // exactly 6 digits, for semi-private
  // maxSize: 10,             // 5 to 10 seats; default 5
  // allowBots: false,        // to keep OTHER bots out of your bot's room
});

  socket.emit("join room", { roomId }); // creating does not seat you
});

Creation is throttled: 10 seconds between rooms per person, 30 seconds and at most 2 standing rooms per IP. An empty room is deleted after 30 seconds, so join what you make.

Joining and sitting down

Node
socket.emit("join room", { roomId: "123456" }); // accessCode: "..." if semi-private

socket.on("room joined", (d) => {
  // d.users            everyone seated, you included
  // d.currentMessages  { userId: "their current box text", ... }
  // d.votes            { voterId: targetUserId, ... }
  // d.roomName, d.roomType, d.layout, d.maxSize, d.locked, d.slowMode
});

You can be in one room at a time. Joining while seated elsewhere is refused until you leave room. A join can also be refused with:

room not found Bad id, or the room is gone. Room ids are 6 digits. room full All seats taken. Its own event, not an error. access code required Semi-private room, no code sent. Resend with accessCode. bot limit Rooms hold 1 bot per 5 seats (a default 5-seat room holds one bot, a 10-seat room two). Arrives as an error. does not allow bots The room was made with bots switched off. banned This identity was vote-kicked from that room earlier. locked / maintenance Staff locked the room, or the whole site is in maintenance mode.
8Talking

Talkomatic has no messages and no send button. Every person owns one textbox, the room watches it change live, and "sending" is just editing your box. You edit yours with chat update diffs:

full-replace { type: "full-replace", text } replaces your whole box. The only one most bots need. add { type: "add", index, text } inserts at a position in your current text. delete { type: "delete", index, count } removes characters. replace { type: "replace", index, text } overwrites characters in place.
Node
let myBox = ""; // your box's current text; the server never echoes it back

// Instant: one event, the whole message.
function say(text) {
  myBox = text;
  socket.emit("chat update", { diff: { type: "full-replace", text } });
}

// A line under what is already there, like the hosted bots' "add a line
// below" block. Oldest lines scroll away as the 5000 character cap nears.
function appendLine(line) {
  let next = myBox ? myBox + "\n" + line : line;
  while (next.length > 4500 && next.includes("\n")) {
    next = next.slice(next.indexOf("\n") + 1);
  }
  say(next);
}

// Typewriter: the hosted bots' pace, 4 letters every 120ms. Each tick is
// one socket event, so keep this for short lines and say() the long ones.
async function type(text) {
  socket.emit("typing", { isTyping: true });
  for (let at = 4; at < text.length + 4; at += 4) {
    say(text.slice(0, at));
    await new Promise((r) => setTimeout(r, 120));
  }
  socket.emit("typing", { isTyping: false });
}

Facts that matter here:

  • Your box holds 5000 characters. Anything past that is cut off.
  • The server never echoes your own edits back, so track your box's text yourself (the appendLine helper above does).
  • The room sees your edits as you make them. A slow trickle of full-replace prefixes looks like typing; the type() helper above matches the hosted bots' pace, 4 letters every 120ms.
  • Each edit is one socket event, and a bot gets 500 events per minute. An instant say() costs 1. A 1000 character typewriter message costs 250. Budget accordingly: typewriter the short lines, say() the walls.
  • typing events ({ isTyping: true }) drive the little typing indicator. Optional, free of the 500 cap, and auto-clears 2 seconds after your last one.
  • Swearing is filtered per viewer by each person's own automod setting, the same as for people. Links get scrubbed out of boxes shortly after they appear (you also get a links not allowed event), and IP addresses are masked for everyone.
9Reading the room

Everyone else's box arrives the same way yours leaves: chat update, on every keystroke. The server always normalizes what you receive to a full-replace carrying the person's entire box, so you never apply diffs yourself:

payload
{ "userId": "abc...", "username": "sara", "diff": { "type": "full-replace", "text": "hello\nis anyone" } }

The catch: with no send button there is no "message received" moment. You decide when a line counts as said. The hosted bots, and the example bots below, wait until a person's last non-empty line has stopped changing for 1.5 seconds, then treat it as spoken, and key it by line number plus text so the same utterance never fires twice:

Node
// Wait until a person's last line has been still for 1.5 seconds, then
// treat it as said. The line number + text key makes one line fire once.
const settleTimers = new Map();
const lastHandled = new Map();

socket.on("chat update", (d) => {
  clearTimeout(settleTimers.get(d.userId));
  settleTimers.set(d.userId, setTimeout(() => {
    const lines = String(d.diff.text || "").split("\n");
    for (let i = lines.length - 1; i >= 0; i--) {
      const line = lines[i].trim();
      if (!line) continue;
      const key = i + ":" + line;
      if (key === lastHandled.get(d.userId)) return;
      lastHandled.set(d.userId, key);
      return onLine(d.userId, d.username, line);
    }
  }, 1500));
});

socket.on("user left", (userId) => {
  settleTimers.delete(userId);
  lastHandled.delete(userId);
});

function onLine(userId, username, line) {
  // line is that person's settled last line. Commands go here.
  if (line.toLowerCase().split(/\s+/).includes("!roll")) {
    say(username + " rolls a " + (1 + Math.floor(Math.random() * 6)) + "!");
  }
}

One honest edge: if a person goes back and extends a settled line ("!roll" into "!roll again"), that is a new key and fires again. The hosted bots behave the same way. People learn to press Enter, and your commands should tolerate a rerun.

The rest of the room reaches you as events:

user joined Someone sat down: a user object plus roomName. Bots carry isBotUser: true, hosted bots also a botOwner name. Check that flag before greeting so two greeters do not greet each other forever. user left Just the departed userId. Clean up any per-user state you keep. user typing { userId, username, isTyping }. update votes { voterId: targetUserId, ... }, the full map each time it changes. Voting needs 3+ people; a majority removes the target. Your bot can vote too: { targetUserId } to place or take back its vote. room update A full room snapshot (same shape as room joined) after bigger changes. error / message error is { error: { code, message } }. message is a soft warning, for example when you edit faster than the chat limiter likes.
10Staying connected (why bots "randomly" vanish)

Nothing about it is random. A bot leaves a room, or loses its socket, for exactly one of these reasons, each with its fix. The two at the top cover nearly every report.

The inactivity check The big one. The server treats anyone whose box has not changed for 2.5 minutes as idle: it sends afk warning, and 30 seconds later afk timeout and removes them from the room. Your own edits reset the clock, so chatty bots never notice, but a bot that mostly listens gets removed 3 minutes after its last word. The socket stays connected; the seat is gone. Fix, one line: socket.on("afk warning", () => socket.emit("afk response")). Handle afk timeout too by rejoining, for the cases below. Server restarts The other big one. Talkomatic deploys and restarts without notice beyond a server restarting event 5 seconds ahead. Your socket drops, and your token died with the restart, so plain reconnects fail with "Invalid bot token" forever. Fix: the auth callback from section 4, plus rejoining your room on connect, makes restarts a 10 second hiccup. The room closed Rooms are not permanent. An empty room lives 30 seconds. A room where one occupant sits alone (your bot counts) is closed after roughly 20 minutes, faster when the server is busy. You get afk timeout with a room-closed message. Fix: recreate or pick a livelier room. Voted out 3+ people, a majority against the bot: it gets kicked, loses the seat, and that identity is banned from that room. The right response is to stay gone, not to reconnect around it. Staff Moderators see every running bot and its owner and can disconnect any of them, or turn all bots off. Repeat offenders collect IP bans. Ping timeout Block your process's event loop for 60+ seconds and the server drops you as dead. Keep heavy work off the socket thread. Connection and event caps 8 sockets per IP, 20 connection attempts per 15 seconds, 500 events per minute per bot. The caps do not disconnect you (events over the line are dropped with an error), but a hot reconnect loop will lock the IP out for 30 seconds at a time.

The whole survival kit is four handlers:

Node
// 1. Answer the inactivity check, or lose the seat 3 minutes after
//    your bot's last word.
socket.on("afk warning", () => socket.emit("afk response"));

// 2. If removed anyway (idle before you added #1, or the room closed),
//    take the seat back.
socket.on("afk timeout", () => {
  setTimeout(() => socket.emit("join room", { roomId }), 1000);
});

// 3. After any reconnect you are signed out and roomless. Walk back in.
//    (With the section 4 auth callback, this also covers server restarts.)
socket.on("connect", () => {
  socket.emit("join lobby", { username: BOT_NAME, location: "Bot" });
});
socket.on("signin status", (s) => {
  if (s.isSignedIn && roomId) socket.emit("join room", { roomId });
});

// 4. Respect a vote-kick. The room said no.
socket.on("kicked", () => process.exit(0));

Everything above in one file that runs as-is: self-renewing token, reconnects, the inactivity handshake, settled-line reading, a typewriter, and two commands. Point TALKOMATIC_SERVER at a test server if you run one; it defaults to the real site.

dicebot.js
// DiceBot: a complete Talkomatic bot.
// Handles its own token, reconnects, survives the inactivity check,
// and answers !roll and !help.
//
//   npm install socket.io-client     (Node 18 or newer)
//   node dicebot.js 123456           (a room id, or none to make a room)

const fs = require("fs");
const { io } = require("socket.io-client");

const SERVER = process.env.TALKOMATIC_SERVER || "https://classic.talkomatic.co";
const BOT_NAME = "DiceBot";
const TOKEN_FILE = "talkomatic-token.json";
const ROOM_ID = process.argv[2] || null;

// ── The token, self-renewing ────────────────────────────────────────────────
// Reuse the saved token while the server still accepts it. If it expired,
// or the server restarted and forgot it, request a fresh one and save it.
async function getToken() {
  try {
    const saved = JSON.parse(fs.readFileSync(TOKEN_FILE, "utf8"));
    const res = await fetch(SERVER + "/api/v1/bot-tokens/info", {
      headers: { Authorization: "Bearer " + saved.token, "User-Agent": BOT_NAME + "/1.0" },
    });
    if (res.ok) return saved.token;
  } catch (err) {}
  const res = await fetch(SERVER + "/api/v1/bot-tokens/request", {
    method: "POST",
    headers: { "User-Agent": BOT_NAME + "/1.0" },
  });
  if (!res.ok) throw new Error("Token request failed: " + res.status + " " + (await res.text()));
  const data = await res.json();
  fs.writeFileSync(TOKEN_FILE, JSON.stringify(data, null, 2));
  console.log("Fresh token saved, good until", data.expiresAt);
  return data.token;
}

// ── Connect ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
// The auth callback runs on every attempt, so a dead token heals itself:
// getToken() checks it against the server and replaces it when refused.
const socket = io(SERVER, {
  transports: ["websocket"],
  auth: (cb) => getToken().then((token) => cb({ token })).catch(() => cb({})),
});

let roomId = ROOM_ID;

socket.on("connect", () => {
  console.log("Connected. Signing in...");
  socket.emit("join lobby", { username: BOT_NAME, location: "Bot" });
});

socket.on("signin status", (s) => {
  if (!s.isSignedIn) return;
  if (roomId) socket.emit("join room", { roomId });
  else socket.emit("create room", { name: BOT_NAME + "'s corner", type: "public" });
});

socket.on("room created", (id) => socket.emit("join room", { roomId: id }));

socket.on("room joined", (d) => {
  roomId = d.roomId;
  console.log("Seated in", d.roomName, "with", d.users.length - 1, "others");
});

socket.on("connect_error", (e) => console.log("Connect refused:", e.message));
socket.on("error", (e) => console.log("Server said:", e?.error?.message || e));
socket.on("kicked", () => { console.log("Voted out. Leaving for good."); process.exit(0); });

// The inactivity check. A bot that only listens looks idle, so the server
// asks after 2.5 quiet minutes and removes anyone silent at 3. Answering
// "afk response" resets the clock. Your own messages also reset it.
socket.on("afk warning", () => socket.emit("afk response"));
socket.on("afk timeout", () => {
  // Removed anyway (or the room closed). Take the seat again.
  console.log("Removed for inactivity, rejoining...");
  setTimeout(() => socket.emit("join room", { roomId }), 1000);
});

// ── Reading the room ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
// There is no send button. Everyone's textbox streams in live, keystroke by
// keystroke, as "chat update" with the person's whole box. Wait until their
// last line has been still for 1.5 seconds, then treat it as said. The
// index:text key makes sure one line only fires once.
const settleTimers = new Map();
const lastHandled = new Map();

socket.on("chat update", (d) => {
  clearTimeout(settleTimers.get(d.userId));
  settleTimers.set(d.userId, setTimeout(() => {
    const lines = String(d.diff.text || "").split("\n");
    for (let i = lines.length - 1; i >= 0; i--) {
      const line = lines[i].trim();
      if (!line) continue;
      const key = i + ":" + line;
      if (key === lastHandled.get(d.userId)) return;
      lastHandled.set(d.userId, key);
      return onLine(d.username, line);
    }
  }, 1500));
});

socket.on("user joined", (u) => say("Welcome, " + u.username + "! Type !roll to roll a die."));
socket.on("user left", (userId) => { settleTimers.delete(userId); lastHandled.delete(userId); });

// ── The bot itself ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
function onLine(username, line) {
  const words = line.toLowerCase().split(/\s+/);
  if (words.includes("!roll")) {
    const n = 1 + Math.floor(Math.random() * 6);
    type(username + " rolls a " + n + "!");
  } else if (words.includes("!help")) {
    say("I know !roll and !help. That's it, I'm a die.");
  }
}

// ── Talking ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
// Your box works like everyone's: send its full text and the room sees it.
function say(text) {
  socket.emit("chat update", { diff: { type: "full-replace", text } });
}

// The typewriter version: same pace as hosted bots (4 letters per 120ms).
// Each tick is one event, so keep long messages for say().
let typeQueue = Promise.resolve();
function type(text) {
  typeQueue = typeQueue.then(async () => {
    socket.emit("typing", { isTyping: true });
    for (let at = 4; at < text.length + 4; at += 4) {
      say(text.slice(0, at));
      await new Promise((r) => setTimeout(r, 120));
    }
    socket.emit("typing", { isTyping: false });
  });
}

Identical shape on python-socketio. The reconnect loop at the bottom re-checks the token on every attempt, which is the Python version of the auth callback:

pricebot.py
# PriceBot: a complete Talkomatic bot in Python.
# Handles its own token, reconnects, survives the inactivity check,
# and answers !price and !help.
#
#   pip install "python-socketio[client]" requests
#   python pricebot.py 123456        (a room id, or none to make a room)

import json
import os
import sys
import threading
import time

import requests
import socketio

SERVER = os.environ.get("TALKOMATIC_SERVER", "https://classic.talkomatic.co")
BOT_NAME = "PriceBot"
TOKEN_FILE = "talkomatic-token.json"
UA = {"User-Agent": BOT_NAME + "/1.0"}

room_id = sys.argv[1] if len(sys.argv) > 1 else None
stopped = False


# ── The token, self-renewing ────────────────────────────────────────────────
# Reuse the saved token while the server still accepts it. If it expired,
# or the server restarted and forgot it, request a fresh one and save it.
def get_token():
    try:
        with open(TOKEN_FILE) as f:
            saved = json.load(f)
        r = requests.get(SERVER + "/api/v1/bot-tokens/info", timeout=10,
                         headers={**UA, "Authorization": "Bearer " + saved["token"]})
        if r.ok:
            return saved["token"]
    except (OSError, ValueError, requests.RequestException):
        pass
    r = requests.post(SERVER + "/api/v1/bot-tokens/request", timeout=10, headers=UA)
    r.raise_for_status()
    data = r.json()
    with open(TOKEN_FILE, "w") as f:
        json.dump(data, f)
    print("Fresh token saved, good until", data["expiresAt"])
    return data["token"]


sio = socketio.Client(reconnection=False)


@sio.event
def connect():
    print("Connected. Signing in...")
    sio.emit("join lobby", {"username": BOT_NAME, "location": "Bot"})


@sio.on("signin status")
def signed_in(s):

        return
    if room_id:
        sio.emit("join room", {"roomId": room_id})
    else:

@sio.on("room joined")
def room_joined(d):
    global room_id
    room_id = d["roomId"]
    print("Seated in", d["roomName"], "with", len(d["users"]) - 1, "others")


@sio.on("error")
def server_error(e):
    print("Server said:", (e.get("error") or {}).get("message", e))


@sio.on("kicked")
def kicked():
    global stopped
    stopped = True
    print("Voted out. Leaving for good.")
    sio.disconnect()


# The inactivity check. A bot that only listens looks idle, so the server
# asks after 2.5 quiet minutes and removes anyone silent at 3. Answering
# "afk response" resets the clock. Your own messages also reset it.
@sio.on("afk warning")
def afk_warning(data):
    sio.emit("afk response")


@sio.on("afk timeout")
def afk_timeout(data):
    print("Removed for inactivity, rejoining...")
    time.sleep(1)
    sio.emit("join room", {"roomId": room_id})


# ── Reading the room ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
# There is no send button. Everyone's textbox streams in live, keystroke by
# keystroke, as "chat update" with the person's whole box. Wait until their
# last line has been still for 1.5 seconds, then treat it as said. The
# index:text key makes sure one line only fires once.
settle_timers = {}
last_handled = {}


@sio.on("chat update")
def chat_update(d):
    user_id = d["userId"]
    old = settle_timers.pop(user_id, None)
    if old:
        old.cancel()
    t = threading.Timer(1.5, settled, args=(user_id, d["username"], d["diff"]["text"]))
    settle_timers[user_id] = t
    t.start()


def settled(user_id, username, text):
    lines = (text or "").split("\n")
    for i in range(len(lines) - 1, -1, -1):
        line = lines[i].strip()
        if not line:
            continue
        key = str(i) + ":" + line
        if last_handled.get(user_id) == key:
            return
        last_handled[user_id] = key
        on_line(username, line)
        return


@sio.on("user joined")
def user_joined(u):
    say("Hello, " + u["username"] + ". Ask me !price.")


@sio.on("user left")
def user_left(user_id):
    settle_timers.pop(user_id, None)
    last_handled.pop(user_id, None)


# ── The bot itself ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
def on_line(username, line):
    words = line.lower().split()
    if "!price" in words:
        say("Fish is 3 coins today, " + username + ". Firm but fair.")
    elif "!help" in words:
        say("I know !price and !help. I am a fish salesman, not a library.")


# Your box works like everyone's: send its full text and the room sees it.
def say(text):
    sio.emit("chat update", {"diff": {"type": "full-replace", "text": text}})


# ── Run forever ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
# get_token() checks the saved token against the server on every attempt,
# so a token that died in a server restart heals itself here.
while not stopped:
    try:
        sio.connect(SERVER, auth={"token": get_token()}, transports=["websocket"])
        sio.wait()
    except (socketio.exceptions.ConnectionError, requests.RequestException) as e:
        print("Connect refused:", e)
    if not stopped:
        time.sleep(3)

15FAQ
My bot connects fine, then disappears from the room after a few minutes. Why?
The inactivity check, nine times out of ten. A bot that has not edited its box for 2.5 minutes gets afk warning; silence for 30 more seconds means afk timeout and the seat is gone, while the socket stays connected, which is exactly why it looks spooky. One line fixes it: socket.on("afk warning", () => socket.emit("afk response")). Section 10 lists every other way a bot leaves a room.
My bot ran for days, then suddenly "Invalid bot token" forever.
The server restarted (updates ship often) and tokens live in server memory, so yours no longer exists. Request a new one. Better: let the bot do it, with the self-renewing token function and auth callback from section 4. Both example bots already include it.
Why "Bot token required" when I am sending one?
The token is not where the server looks. On the socket it must be auth: { token: "tk_..." } in the connection options. On HTTP it is an Authorization: Bearer tk_... header. A common miss is putting it in query under another name, or sending the whole {"token": ...} JSON blob instead of the bare string.
My command fires twice, or fires while the person is still typing.
You are reacting to raw chat update events, which arrive per keystroke. Debounce: wait until the person's last line has been still for about 1.5 seconds and key each utterance by line number plus text, like section 9. Note that a settled line edited again is a genuinely new utterance and fires again; hosted bots work the same way.
I emit "chat update" and nothing appears.
In order of likelihood: you are not in a room yet (wait for room joined); the diff shape is wrong, which answers with an error event you should be logging; you spent the 500 events per minute budget, also an error; or you are watching your own box for the echo, which never comes, since the server only broadcasts your edits to others.
Can my bot join any room?
Public rooms with a free seat and a free bot slot, yes. Semi-private needs the 6-digit code. Private needs the room id from whoever made it. Refusals to expect: full, locked, bots not allowed, bot slots taken (1 per 5 seats), banned after a vote-kick, maintenance mode. Section 7 has the whole list.
How do I run more than one bot?
One connection per bot, each signed in with its own name. One token can carry several connections. The walls: 8 sockets per IP, one bot slot per 5 seats in any one room, and one room per identity. Two of your bots can share a 10-seat room, since it has two bot slots.
Do bots see other bots? Will mine loop with a hosted bot?
Your bot receives everything, hosted bots included, and hosted bots can hear yours. Loops are real: two greeters can greet each other forever. Check isBotUser on user joined and on the users list, remember those ids, and stay quiet at anything they say. (Hosted bots ignore other bots automatically; that protection is yours to build.)
Is there a sandbox to develop against?
Make yourself a private room (type: "private"): it never shows in the lobby, and only people with the id can wander in. Remember a room with just your bot in it closes after ~20 minutes; recreating it on afk timeout is enough for a test loop. You can also run the open source server locally and point your bot's server URL at it, which is how the examples here were tested.
Why did my message get stars in it? Why did my link vanish?
Stars are each viewer's own automod choice, applied in their browser, the same as for human text; you cannot turn it off for them. Links are scrubbed server-side shortly after they appear in any box, with a links not allowed event to the sender, and IP addresses are masked. Design your bot's output around that: no link menus.
Does my bot need to send typing indicators?
No. Its edits are visible live either way. The indicator is cosmetic polish: hosted bots light it while their typewriter runs, and type() in the Node example does the same. If you send it, remember to send { isTyping: false } when done.
Can a bot have a profile picture?
Yes: avatar: { preset: n } on join lobby, with valid n values from GET /api/v1/avatars (Bearer token required). Invalid presets are silently dropped and the bot shows the default silhouette.
What language should I use?
Anything with a Socket.IO v4 client: socket.io-client on Node, python-socketio on Python, and there are maintained clients for Go, Rust, Java, C# and more. The protocol on this page is the same in all of them: connect with the token, join lobby, join room, then trade chat update events.
